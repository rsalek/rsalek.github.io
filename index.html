About

Reza has two decades of experience in metabolomics research and development, in both the wet lab and computational work. He advises on metabolic biomarker discovery, analytical methods (NMR, LC-MS and GC-MS), data science and clinical biostatistics, high-throughput laboratory automation, and regulatory frameworks including GLP, ISO and IVD.

He is a Translational Research Programme Manager at the University of Cambridge’s Office for Translational Research, in the School of Clinical Medicine, specialising in MedTech and medical devices — including software, apps, and AI as medical devices. He is Section Editor-in-Chief for Integrative Metabolomics at the journal Metabolites, and is based at the Cambridge Biomedical Campus.

BiotechVision’s consultancy work has included academic institutes, international organisations, pharma and biotech companies, and startups, with advice offered from study design through to data analysis.

Services

The consultancy provides advice across metabolomics, lipidomics and exposomics, including the areas below.

Biomarker strategies

Planning metabolomics work for biomarker discovery, including markers of disease, drug response, or personalised nutrition, and guidance through the analysis of the resulting data.

Translational insight

Help mapping a project from laboratory research towards clinical use, including metabolomic signatures relevant to disease progression, treatment effect, and therapeutic decisions.

Workflow design

Design and implementation of custom targeted and untargeted metabolomics or lipidomics workflows, including instrument choice, method development, and integration into existing research infrastructure.

External partnerships

Help finding and working with external laboratories and contract research organisations for sample analysis, data interpretation, or specialist expertise.

Analytical approaches

Advice covers the choice and use of GC-MS, LC-MS and NMR, and the design of targeted, semi-targeted or untargeted experiments. Support is also available for laboratory automation, from sample handling through to data-processing pipelines.

GC-MS

High sensitivity and resolution, particularly for volatile compounds; less suitable for non-volatile analytes and may require derivatisation.

LC-MS

Broad coverage of polar and non-polar molecules; sample preparation can be complex, and matrix effects need careful handling.

NMR

Non-destructive analysis with quantitative and structural information; lower sensitivity than mass spectrometry, and limited to NMR-active nuclei.
